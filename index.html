Worth knowing: HDB looks backwards

The income HDB uses is your gross monthly household income, averaged over the months worked in a 12-month window that ends 2 months before the month you apply for the HFE letter. Everyone listed in the application is included. HDB counts gross pay including your own CPF deduction, and leaves out employer CPF contributions and bonuses.

That makes it a look-back, not a forecast. If you have only just started work, one of you is still studying, or a second income only begins after you apply, the figure HDB uses can be well below what you will earn later.

It cuts both ways. The EHG is means-tested, so a lower assessed income lands in a higher grant band. But the same income also limits the loan HDB will offer, through the 30% income rule in step 7.

Two rules limit this. The EHG needs you or your spouse to have worked continuously for the 12 months before the HFE application. A couple who were both full-time students or National Servicemen in that period cannot get the EHG yet, so HDB lets them apply for a flat first and defer the income assessment until they are in continuous employment. A couple where one partner has already worked for those 12 months can qualify without deferring. (Minister for National Development, Parliament, 2 Jul 2024.)

Sources: HDB on how it works out average household income, HDB HFE letter FAQ, CPF Board EHG guide, MND written answer on deferred income assessment.

How CPF splits between two owners

There's no fixed order, and it isn't based on whose account is bigger. Each co-owner declares what share of the instalment comes from their own CPF OA when the deduction is set up — commonly equal, but it doesn't have to be. The one hard rule: the combined deduction across all owners can't exceed the instalment actually due.

That declared share also sets each owner's required Home Protection Scheme cover, and either of you can ask HDB to change the split later.

Sources: CPF Board on the combined instalment cap, CPF Board on the declared HPS/instalment share, HDB on varying your CPF contribution rate.
